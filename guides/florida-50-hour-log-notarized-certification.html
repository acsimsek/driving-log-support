Florida 50-hour driving log and the notarized certification

Checked against FLHSMV's driving log on 2026-07-31.

50supervised hours required
10of those hours at night
Florida's certification is sworn: the 50 hours are attested before a notary or a license examiner. That is why the day/night split matters from the first drive; the number you swear to should be one you can back up with dated entries.

The rule in three lines

Supervised practice50 hours, of which 10 at night
Learner license windowdaylight only for the first 3 months, so night hours come later in the year
Supervising adultage 21+

The paperwork: Learner's License Driving Log + form 71143 (signed before a notary or a license examiner)

The state's own log sheet classifies each drive simply as day or night. Keep that split as you go, print the log, and bring it with the certification form to be signed in front of the notary or examiner. The printout from an app is a supporting record; the sworn certification is what the state accepts.

Planning the night hours

Because the first 3 months are daylight-only, families who start in spring often reach 40 daytime hours quickly and then stall on the 10 night hours. Put a few evening drives on the calendar as soon as the daylight-only period ends.

What an app should do for Florida

  • Keep night minutes separate and show how many of the 10 remain.
  • Warn about night entries dated inside the daylight-only window.
  • Print a dated record you can attach to the certification. No app produces the official form.

Driving Log does these for Florida and links the rule to its source. See the Florida guide for the full summary.
