Georgia 40-hour driving log: do you need one under Joshua's Law?

For the teen learner-permit path. Rule data verified 2026-09-21; linked paperwork checked 17 October 2026.

40supervised hours required
6of those hours at night
12 calendar months plus 1 daydefault supervised period
Georgia teens need 40 hours of supervised driving, 6 of them at night, and must hold the learner's permit for 1 year and 1 day. DDS does not require a driving log. Instead, a parent or guardian comes to the Class D application and swears or affirms, under penalty of law, that the hours were completed.

No log required, but the oath is real

Georgia DDS says plainly that a driving log is not required for supervised driving. What it does require is the parent or guardian at the counter: according to the DDS teen driving FAQ, a parent, guardian or DDS-certified driver education instructor swears or affirms under penalty of law that the applicant received the 40 hours, 6 at night.

That is a sworn number with nothing behind it unless you keep a record. A dated log is what lets a parent sign with confidence, and the only way to show that 6 of the hours really were at night.

Joshua's Law: four ways to finish driver education

Joshua's Law also requires an approved driver education course. DDS lists four methods; finish every item in the one you choose.

MethodInstructionBehind the wheel
130 h classroom6 h with a certified school, plus 40 h supervised with a parent or guardian (6 at night)
230 h classroom40 h parent-taught with the DDS Parent/Teen Driving Guide, which includes the 40 supervised hours
330 h onlineSame as Method 1
430 h onlineSame as Method 2

Since 1 July 2021, 17-year-olds applying for a Class D meet the same requirements as 16-year-olds. Check the DDS Joshua's Law page for the current list of approved schools and online programs.

Who can supervise

Someone at least 21 years old with an unexpired Class C license, sitting next to the learner and able to take control of the vehicle if needed.

What an app should do for Georgia

  • Keep night minutes separate and show how many of the 6 remain.
  • Count 1 year and 1 day from the permit date, not just 12 months.
  • Print a dated record to bring to DDS. DDS does not ask for it; it backs up the parent's statement. No app produces an official Georgia form.

Mistakes that make the oath hard to give

  • Estimating 40 hours at the counter instead of adding up dated drives.
  • Reaching 40 hours with fewer than 6 at night.
  • Applying for the Class D a day early: the permit must be held 1 year and 1 day.
  • Arriving without the parent or guardian, who must be present to attest.
  • Assuming the 6 instructor hours in Method 1 replace the 40 supervised hours. They are separate items.
