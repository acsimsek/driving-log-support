Ohio 50-hour affidavit (BMV 5791): log, notary and night hours

For the teen learner-permit path. Rule data verified 2026-09-21; linked paperwork checked 17 October 2026.

Ohio teens need 50 hours of supervised driving, at least 10 of them at night, and must hold the temporary permit (TIPIC) for 6 months. The hours are recorded on the BMV 5791 Fifty Hour Affidavit, which a parent or guardian signs in front of a notary.

The affidavit is the log

Form BMV 5791 Fifty Hour Affidavit (revision 9/25) has two parts: a supervised driving log, split into 40 daytime and 10 nighttime hours, and an affidavit that an eligible adult signs before a notary public. The eligible adult is the parent, guardian or custodian, or a person over 21 acting in loco parentis.

The form names two ways to document the hours: fill in its own log page, or use the RoadReady Ohio app. If you keep the log in another app, copy the dated entries onto the form's log page before you see the notary. Bring the notarized form to the driving skills test and to the Deputy Registrar when you buy the license; Deputy Registrar agencies have notaries.

What counts as night in Ohio

BMV 5791 defines nighttime as starting half an hour after sunset and ending half an hour before sunrise. The first 30 minutes after sunset are daytime on the form.

Who can supervise

According to the Digest of Ohio Motor Vehicle Laws, a 16- or 17-year-old permit holder may practise with any licensed driver aged 21 or older, except from midnight to 6 a.m., when the supervisor must be a parent, guardian or custodian with a valid license. Under 16, the supervisor must be a parent, guardian, custodian, licensed driving instructor or a person 21 or older acting in loco parentis, in the front passenger seat.

Driver education, and ages 18 to 20

Teens under 18 also complete 24 hours of classroom or online instruction and 8 hours of driving with a licensed instructor. Since 30 September 2025, Ohioans aged 18, 19 and 20 need the same 50 hours, 10 at night, with a licensed driver 21 or older, plus a Class D driver training course; they do not have to hold the permit for 6 months.

Mistakes that get the affidavit sent back

  • Signing the affidavit at home; the eligible adult signs it in front of the notary.
  • Counting the first half hour after sunset as night.
  • A midnight drive supervised by someone other than a parent, guardian or custodian.
  • Bringing only an app printout instead of the BMV 5791 with its log page filled in.
  • An 18-, 19- or 20-year-old assuming the 50 hours no longer apply to them. Since September 2025 they do.
