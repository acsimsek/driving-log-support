Pennsylvania 65-hour driving log: DL-180C and bad-weather hours

For the teen learner-permit path. Rule data verified 2026-09-21; linked paperwork checked 17 October 2026.

Pennsylvania requires 65 hours of supervised practice, including 10 at night and 5 in bad weather, and at least 6 months on the learner's permit before the road test. A parent or guardian certifies the hours on form DL-180C.

The log and the certification are different papers

PennDOT's parent guide, The Parent's Supervised Driving Program (PUB 385A), includes a supervised driving log with columns for the date, day or night, weather, skills practised and driving time. Keep it, or an equally detailed record, as you go.

The certification is the DL-180C Parent or Guardian Certification Form. A parent, guardian, person in loco parentis or spouse aged 18 or older signs that the learner completed at least 65 hours, including 10 at night and 5 in inclement weather, and has seen PennDOT's distracted-driving material. It is signed in front of a notary, a driver license examiner or one of the other officials listed on the form; a notarized signature is needed only when the parent or guardian is not going with the teen to the Driver License Center for the skills test.

Who can supervise

A licensed driver aged 21 or older, or a licensed parent, guardian, person in loco parentis, or spouse aged 18 or older, seated beside the learner. PennDOT's parent guide adds that the supervisor must be licensed for the same or a higher class of vehicle than the permit. The DL-180C's wording certifies practice given by licensed drivers aged 21 or older; if a supervising parent or spouse is younger, ask PennDOT how to certify those hours.

The DL-180C does not say whether one rainy drive after dark counts toward both minimums. Record day or night and the weather separately for every drive, so either total can be shown.

  • Print a dated record to keep with the PUB 385A log. No app produces the DL-180C itself.

Mistakes that hold up the road test

  • Reaching 65 hours without 10 at night or 5 in bad weather.
  • Writing the weather only into a final total; without dated entries the 5 hours are hard to show.
  • Planning the road test without counting 6 months from the permit issue date.
  • A parent who cannot come to the skills test, and a DL-180C that was never notarized.
  • Treating an app printout as the DL-180C. The printout supports the certification; it does not replace it.
