Texas 30-hour driving log: what actually counts

Checked against TDLR's form and guide on 2026-07-31.

Most Texas families find out late that only 2 hours per day count toward the 30. A four-hour road trip is good practice, but it credits two hours on the log. Spread practice across at least 30 different days.

The five numbers

Supervised practice30 hours, of which 10 at night
Daily credit cap2 hours per day
Separate practice daysat least 30
Learner license heldat least 6 months
Supervising adultage 21+, licensed 1+ years, signs each entry

The 30 hours are not the whole in-car requirement

The 30-hour log covers supervised practice. Texas driver education also has an in-car phase of 7 hours behind the wheel plus 7 hours of in-car observation with the instructor, and in parent-taught driver education (PTDE) the parent instructor is that instructor. Provider guides describe the combined requirement as 44 hours; the log form itself covers the 30. Confirm the current split with your course provider and the TDLR guide linked below.

The form: TDLR Form DES150N — Driver Education 30 Hour Behind the Wheel Log

The parent or guardian certifies the whole log and signs with their driver licence number; a student over 18 signs for themselves. Each entry also carries the supervising adult's signature and DL number.

Fields the form asks for: Student's name, DL #, Date, Time (am/pm), Daytime hours, Nighttime hours, Adult's signature and DL #. Keep the date, the am/pm time and the day/night split for every drive as you go; reconstructing them at the end is where logs get rejected.

What an app should do for Texas

  • Apply the 2-hour daily cap automatically and show both the time you drove and the time that counts.
  • Keep night minutes separate from day minutes.
  • Count distinct practice days toward the 30-day minimum.
  • Print a dated record you can copy onto, or attach to, the TDLR form. No app produces the official form itself.

Driving Log does these four things for Texas and links the rule to its source. The full rule summary is on the Texas guide.
